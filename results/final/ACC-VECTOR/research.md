# ACC-VECTOR research

Sources were downloaded with `curl -L` on 2026-11-15 and read locally (HTML or pdfminer.six text). No source code was copied.

- [CubiCasa5K, Kalervo et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01920), including its [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.01920): treats rooms, icons and openings as distinct floor-plan analysis tasks, with polygon annotations and icon categories including sanitary fixtures. We implemented deterministic CAD icon grouping, not its neural network. Our inspected data contained 203 enclosing rectangles across 12 eligible Swiss drawings, including 81 bowl curves and 30 rounded tub curves; this is local evidence, not a reproduction of the paper's benchmark.
- [Raster-to-Vector: Revisiting Floorplan Transformation, Liu et al., ICCV 2017](https://jiajunwu.com/papers/im2cad_iccv.pdf): despite the `im2cad` filename, the downloaded PDF's actual title/authors are Raster-to-Vector / Liu, Wu, Kohli and Furukawa. It combines predicted junctions with integer programming to assemble consistent wall, opening and icon primitives. We use containment and closed-frame constraints on existing CAD vectors; no neural predictor or integer program was implemented. On 50 eligible real-data questions, exact count answers increased from 24 to 36; door/window answers stayed 24/24.
- [Detecting Doors from Swing Arc Blocks](https://indoor-mapping-wayfinding.com/automated-floor-plan-parsing-vectorization/wall-door-detection-algorithms/detecting-doors-from-swing-arc-blocks/): its original engineering article describes leaf/arc/block signatures, warns that fillets resemble door arcs, and discusses grouping double doors. We count closed source frame objects and explicit opening blocks, never arcs alone. All 24 measured door/window answers stayed correct; synthetic checks cover arc-only fillets, a double-door block with two separate leaf frames, and preserving distinct coincident source entities. Its illustrative numerical claims were not adopted as evidence or thresholds. The web tool could not open this page; `curl` successfully retrieved and we read the full article.

All four anatomy thresholds are midpoints between measured distributions in `constants.json`, with per-source geometry in `measurements.json`. Tub vertex counts were measured (69 raw vertices), but the classifier does not require 69: it recognises a rounded elongated closed contour with a drain. Recognition scores are explicitly uncalibrated estimates, not measured probabilities. Kitchen rings are not evidence of a sink; crossed boxes without drains remain uncertain.

Generality limit: eligible native DXF data here has only two dataset origins, Swiss Dwellings and Opening Design. The latter files test safe fallback, not sanitary classification transfer. A third independent DXF origin remains unverified. Raster extraction is unchanged. PDF ingestion was not edited; `classify_fixture_shapes(shapes: list[VectorShape]) -> list[dict]` is the clean hook for Awais.

Final blind checks (reserved before development, opened only after implementation froze): the Swiss file's recognised WC/bathtub/shower counts matched all three available source counts, but one uncertain symbol made the existing solver abstain on those questions. The door/window answers remained correct: overall 2/5 before and after, not an answer-accuracy gain. The other blind file safely used the existing fallback with no spurious fixtures. Nothing was tuned after these checks. The matched fresh-ingestion sample200 run improved 137/187 to 142/187 numeric questions, with zero previously correct answers lost and zero model spend.
